# More precise Search by meaning on a small host

Start with candidate retrieval. On the 34 new semantic questions, PR #14 placed a labeled ayah in the first five for 7 questions and in the first ten for 8. Even perfect reordering of those ten could recover only one extra known hit. Other relevant ayat may be missing from the labels. These counts come from intersecting each question's expected references with its saved browser ranking. [Questions](semantic_followup.jsonl) · [Rankings](semantic_browser_results.json)

Keep the reader's explicit meaning mode, one query embedding per search and zero model inference in Flask. Use PR #14's int8 E5-small browser worker and 6,236 English translations as the control. Its static index is about 2.4 MB. The first use transferred about 140 MB and took about 50 seconds on the test desktop. The 70 ms warm p95 measured queries within an already loaded worker. [Baseline report](SEMANTIC_FOLLOWUP.md)

## Experiments in order

| Experiment | What to compare | What would justify pursuing it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Index questions an ayah can answer | Offline, write or generate up to two distinct questions grounded in each translation. For 24:27, one could be “What should I do before entering someone else's house?” Embed each as a separate passage with E5-small. Search all vectors once, keep the highest score per ayah, and return unique ayat. Compare translation alone, translation plus one question, then plus two. Cap each ayah equally where evidence permits. | More distinct topics with a relevant ayah in the first 50, followed by better judged precision in the first five. Three 384-dimensional int8 vectors per ayah would use about 7.2 MB of raw vectors. The query model stays the same. This adapts the offline question-generation idea from [Doc2Query++ (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09557). |
| 2. Teach the small model to distinguish close verses | Save the first 50 unique ayat for each development question. Score the pairs offline with Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B. Compare its ordering on that exact list with E5's and the best ordering permitted by the judgments. If it adds precision, train multilingual E5-small offline on training-topic pairs to imitate those scores, including tempting wrong answers. Export the student to int8, rebuild passage vectors, then benchmark the shipped browser model. | The student recovers a measurable share of the teacher's gain at the existing model size and improves held-out rankings. Human reviewers should check close negatives, since several ayat can legitimately answer a question. [Qwen reranker and usage](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B) |
| 3. Shrink the reader's download | If device tests identify model loading as the bottleneck, train a smaller multilingual query encoder to match a frozen stronger passage encoder. Compute passage embeddings offline and train the query side into that same vector space. Compare with a symmetric strong encoder offline and with the best browser candidate. Test the full tokenizer and int8 export on a phone. | A materially smaller cold transfer and shorter time to first result with comparable judged precision in English, Bangla and Arabic. The published asymmetric approach demonstrates feasibility on other data. Aayatun needs its own measurements. [Efficient online query encoding](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.4/) |

## Evaluation and decision

1. **Diagnose the baseline.** Extend the evaluation harness to retain the first 50 unique verses. Count questions with a known relevant verse at ranks 1–5, 6–10, 11–50 and outside 50. This distinguishes failures to retrieve a candidate from failures to order it. Use the existing 186 queries for development and historical comparison. The 90 older non-English queries repeat two topics.
2. **Build a blind test.** Collect fresh, independently written English, Bangla and Arabic questions, including indirectly worded situations, negation and questions with several valid answers. Keep all translations and paraphrases of one intent in the same split. Lock the test before generating training questions, exclude test prompts from generation, and check for near-duplicates. Blindly grade pooled top-ten verses from the control and finalists as direct, partial or irrelevant. Have a second reader resolve disputed judgments using the verse and its context. Record a short reason.
3. **Compare precision and coverage.** Primary metric: the fraction of the first five verses judged directly relevant (precision@5). Also report graded nDCG@5, questions with any direct hit in the first five, and known-answer coverage at 50. Publish per-language wins, losses and paired 95% intervals resampled by intent. Choose the winner on development data and confirm once on the locked test. If uncertainty includes no gain, collect more independent intents before promoting it.
4. **Measure the cost people experience.** On the same desktop and a physical midrange phone, record cold bytes, peak memory, full time to first visible result, repeat search after navigation, and warm p50/p95. Separate encoding from scanning. Compare original and expanded indices with an exact scan. If real additions such as translations or tafsir make scanning dominate, trial approximate nearest-neighbor retrieval against exact results on that same corpus. Report candidate overlap at 50, judged precision, index size and startup time.
5. **Promotion.** Proposed first-experiment target: at least five percentage points higher precision@5 on the locked set, a positive lower bound on the paired overall interval, and no observed hit@5 loss on the established English, Bangla or Arabic slices. Flag underpowered slices explicitly. Keep server inference at zero, measure cold start against the control on the same phone, and replay PR #1's keyword, phrase, surah and root regression tests before proposing a production change.
